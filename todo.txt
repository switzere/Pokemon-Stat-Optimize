The Problem (0:45 - 1:45)

Show a Pokémon stat screen.

"At first glance, defensive optimization seems straightforward.

The amount of damage a Pokémon takes depends on two major defensive stats:

HP
Defense

Increasing either one improves survivability.

The challenge is that EVs are limited.

If I spend EVs on HP, I can't spend them on Defense.

So every defensive Pokémon faces an optimization problem.

Given a fixed number of EVs, where should they go?"

Show EV budget graphic.

"This is exactly the kind of resource allocation problem that data science is built to solve."

Show formula animation from Manim.

"The first step was modeling the actual Pokémon damage formula.

Damage depends on:

Attack stat
Defense stat
Move power
Multipliers like STAB and type effectiveness
Several rounding operations

For this project I focused on the defensive side of the equation.

Once level and attack values are fixed, we can see that incoming damage is roughly inversely proportional to Defense."

"But HP works differently.

Building the Model (3:00 - 4:00)

Show notebook.

"I wrote a Python model that calculates the percentage of HP lost from an attack.

For every possible EV allocation, the program:

Calculates final HP
Calculates final Defense
Applies the Pokémon damage formula
Converts damage into percent health lost

Then it repeats that process for every legal EV combination."

Show code snippets.

"This effectively brute-forces the entire search space."

Visualizing the Search Space (4:00 - 5:30)

Show 3D surface graph.

"Once I generated all possible EV spreads, I visualized them as a surface.

Each point represents a unique HP and Defense allocation.

The height represents damage taken.

Lower is better."

Pause.

"What surprised me was the shape of the surface.

The relationship isn't linear.

Some investments provide significantly more value than others."

Transition to heatmap.

"The heatmap makes this even easier to see.

Instead of randomly guessing EV spreads, we can literally see where survivability is maximized."

The Hidden Part: Breakpoints (5:30 - 7:00)

Show damage difference graph.

"Then I discovered something even more interesting.

Pokémon damage calculations use integer rounding.

That means survivability doesn't improve smoothly.

Instead, it improves in jumps."

Show graph.

"Notice how some Defense investments do absolutely nothing.

Then suddenly a single EV causes damage to drop by an entire point.

Competitive players often call these breakpoints."

Highlight a breakpoint.

"This means two EV spreads that look almost identical can actually perform very differently in battle."

Expanding the Problem (7:00 - 8:30)

Show multiple attacks.

"Optimizing against one attack is useful.

But VGC isn't played against one attack.

You're facing an entire metagame.

So I expanded the model.

Instead of optimizing for a single move, I created a list of relevant attacks and defensive benchmarks.

The algorithm searches for EV spreads that survive all of them simultaneously."

Show stacked EV chart.

"This transforms the problem from simple optimization into a constrained optimization problem."

Results (8:30 - 9:30)

"This produced something I found really interesting.

The optimal EV spread wasn't always what intuition suggested.

In some situations, investing heavily into Defense was best.

In others, HP became more efficient.

And once multiple attacks were considered, the solution changed again."

Show final results graph.

"The important takeaway isn't one specific EV spread.

It's that we can systematically discover optimal spreads instead of guessing."
